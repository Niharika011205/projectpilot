500 Seats AI Worklog

AI worklog · NxtWave Growth Associate challenge

How AI shaped the plan and the prototype

Three iterations from building the “500 seats on ₹2,000” campaign. Each one shows the prompt, what the AI produced, what changed and why, and what shipped.

ProjectPilot prototype (live)

Example 1 · Growth plan

From a long plan to a 5-slide deck

  1. Prompt

    Act as a world-class Growth Marketing Strategist… Budget Allocation (₹2,000): an innovative breakdown beyond standard social ads… Multi-Channel Acquisition Plan… 7-Day Campaign Timeline: day-by-day execution strategy from launch to workshop day.

  2. AI output

    A full plan of roughly 3,000 words: persona, a 5-line budget table, 6 channels with seat targets, a day-by-day timeline, 3 prototype concepts, reflection notes and a video script.

  3. What I changed or rejected
    • The submission allows 5 slides, so each slide got one idea. Persona and insight merged; budget and channels became one table with a seat target per row.
    • Rejected spending the budget on Meta ads. At an estimated ₹15–40 per student lead, ₹2,000 buys 50–130 seats, not 500.
    • Labelled the ad cost as an estimate and the funnel numbers as targets, because the campaign has not run.
    • Corrected the company name to NxtWave everywhere.
  4. Final output

    A 5-slide deck: the ₹4-per-seat constraint, the audience insight, a results-based budget, the 7-day timeline and the working asset.

Example 2 · The AI inside the product

Making the project generator demo-proof

  1. Prompt

    You design beginner AI projects for final-year Indian engineering students… buildable in 60 minutes… Return ONLY valid JSON: {title, tagline, problem, tools, steps, resume_bullet, wow_factor}

  2. AI output

    A working first prompt and schema, with nothing checking the reply. A model can return 3 or 6 steps, suggest paid tools, or send broken JSON, and one failed call during a live demo breaks the whole flow.

  3. What I changed or rejected
    • Added hard rules: free tools only, no GPU training, exactly 4 steps that add up to 60 minutes, a real Indian context.
    • Added a check that rejects incomplete replies, plus 12 hand-written starter projects matched by branch and interest as a fallback.
    • Reframed the output as a lab record (Aim, Apparatus, Procedure, Result), a format every engineering student already knows.
    • Rejected a resume-scoring tool as the prototype: a low score discourages students, and uploading resumes raises privacy concerns.
  4. Final output

    The generator always returns a usable project in seconds: written by AI when available, a matched starter project otherwise.

Example 3 · Prototype build

From build spec to a tested, working page

  1. Prompt

    Pick the single best concept and provide a full code snippet/specification or step-by-step instructions on how to build it using prompt-based web builders.

  2. AI output

    A Lovable + Supabase spec: database schema, an edge function that calls an LLM, and invite links in the form ?ref=CODE.

  3. What I changed or rejected
    • Built the demo as a hosted page reviewers can open with no setup. The Supabase spec stays as the production path.
    • The host drops query strings, so invite links moved from ?ref=CODE to #r-CODE.
    • Rejected a full WhatsApp Business API bot: Meta verification and template approval take longer than 7 days. Used free one-tap share links instead.
    • Phone numbers and emails are readable only by the organiser; the leaderboard shows first name and college.
    • A scripted test of the whole loop (Sai registers, Priya joins through Sai’s link, Sai moves to #1) caught the word “null” printing on the project sheet. Fixed and retested.
  4. Final output

    ProjectPilot: a live project generator with seat registration, WhatsApp invites, a referral leaderboard and an organiser view.

AI was fastest at first drafts and code. The changes that mattered came from limits it didn’t know about: a 5-slide cap, how the host handles links, what students recognise, and what breaks in a live demo.
